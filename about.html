About

I started building PCs at 13. The infrastructure came from trying to make ordinary things work properly.

WHERE I AM NOW

I work in mining today.

I work in mining and am moving toward network engineering and site reliability. I would rather show the systems I run than borrow a title I do not hold yet.

The work at home now covers networking, compute, storage, monitoring, recovery and the small automations that keep all of it from becoming a second full-time job.

WHY THE LAB EXISTS

It began with subscriptions.

The homelab started as a way out of more than $200 a month in hosted services. The first versions were for me. That changed when friends and family began using them too.

Twenty-nine people use the lab, twenty-three regularly. Over the past year they logged 5,769 hours across services running on infrastructure I built and maintain.

WHAT CHANGED

Then other people started using it.

A Raspberry Pi became a Linux host, then a Proxmox cluster. An ASUS mesh became UniFi networking. Old family VHS tapes filled eight terabytes and turned storage and recovery into something that could not be hand-waved.
